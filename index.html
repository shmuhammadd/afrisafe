Making Africa
safe in AI.

AfriSafe studies how AI systems fail in African languages and cultures. We train African researchers to find those failures and fix them.

AI safety work stops at English

Safety tests are written in English

A model that refuses a harmful request in English may answer the same request in Hausa or Zulu. Few labs test for it.

Alignment assumes Western values

Models misread African norms on family, religion and respect. They give advice that fits London and fails in Kano.

Few African researchers work on safety

The people who speak these languages are missing from the teams that test the models.

What we work on

Red teaming in African languages

We attack models in the languages their makers did not test. Native speakers write the prompts, so the attacks use real slang, proverbs and code-switching.

Cultural alignment

We measure how well models know everyday life across African communities, and where their answers carry another culture's assumptions.

Safety benchmarks

We build open test sets for hate speech, toxicity and harmful advice in African languages, labelled by people who speak them.

Alignment of multilingual models

We study why safety training in English fails to carry over to other languages, and test methods that close the gap.

Policy and governance

We write briefs for African regulators who must decide how AI systems are tested before they reach the public.

Training new researchers

The AfriSafe Fellowship takes people who know these languages and teaches them to test AI systems.

The AfriSafe Fellowship

Train as an AI safety researcher, working on your own languages.

Register interest and we will email you

  1. 1. Apply

    Send a short application and a research idea. We look for people who know an African language and can code.

  2. 2. Train

    Study red teaming, evaluation and alignment through taught sessions and paper readings.

  3. 3. Research

    Run a project with a mentor. Test a real model in a real language and record what breaks.

  4. 4. Publish

    Release the paper, the dataset or the benchmark openly, so labs and regulators can use it.

Questions people ask

What is AfriSafe?

AfriSafe is a research group working on AI safety for Africa. We test AI models in African languages, publish what we find, and train new researchers through a fellowship.

When do fellowship applications open?

We will announce the date on this page. Register your interest by email and we will tell you first.

Who is the fellowship for?

Students and early-career researchers who know at least one African language and can program. You do not need earlier work in AI safety.

Do I need a PhD?

No. We care about what you can do and what you want to study.

Which languages do you cover?

Any African language. Our published work so far covers Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Swahili, Amharic and more than a dozen others.
